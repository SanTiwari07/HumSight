# COOL (Cloud-Optimized OpenCV) evidence sheet

For the **Best Use of COOL** award the competition asks for: the COOL version and AWS instance configuration, a reproducible evaluation method with inputs, baselines and results, and evidence that COOL executes the claimed core workload on the Arm path.

**Status: run on 24 Oct 2026.** COOL executed the whole HumSight pipeline on a Graviton4 instance, and we compared it with stock OpenCV on the same machine. **The result is a null result: we measured no meaningful speed-up on this workload**, and we do not claim one. The numbers below are generated (`bench/RESULTS.md`); nothing is retyped.

## Claimed core workload and method

The workload is the HumSight pipeline: per-patch sub-pixel motion (`phaseCorrelateIterative`), spectra, optional stabilisation (`calcOpticalFlowPyrLK`, `findHomography`, `warpPerspective`) and ROI magnification (`pyrDown`/`pyrUp`). `bench/bench.py` times these stages, `load+downscale`, `stabilise(warp)`, `patch_shifts(coarse)`, `patch_shifts(fine)`, `spectra`, `magnify(ROI)`, `encode`, plus micro-benchmarks of individual OpenCV functions.

- **Inputs:** two seeded synthetic clips (clean, and with camera shake), 480×270, 2 s at 240 fps (480 frames), written once as `mp4v` so that `load` includes a real decode. The default 640×360, 4 s clip needs more than the instance's 3.8 GB of RAM: the kernel killed the first attempt (out of memory), so the size was reduced and recorded in the JSON.
- **Protocol:** 1 warm-up and 7 timed runs, `cv2.setNumThreads` 1 and 2, median and IQR, COOL and stock in sequence on the same instance, nothing else running.
- **Common codec:** the COOL build offers only `mp4v` (no `avc1`, no `VP80`) while the stock wheel also has `VP80`, so the benchmark forces `mp4v` in both (`HUMSIGHT_FORCE_CODEC`); otherwise the encode stage would time different work. Codec availability is recorded in `bench/cool.json` and `bench/stock.json`.

## Results

Totals across stages were **0.98x to 1.03x** (stock time divided by COOL time, so above 1 favours COOL; within the run-to-run spread) for both clips and both thread counts. In the micro-benchmarks `resize(INTER_AREA)` was **1.42x faster** on COOL, `cvtColor` was **17% slower** (0.83x), and `GaussianBlur`, `pyrDown`, `pyrUp`, `Sobel`, `findContours`, `phaseCorrelate*` and `warpPerspective` were within about ±8% (`warpPerspective` 0.92x to 0.97x). **Why so little difference (hypotheses, not measured):** the pipeline is dominated by `phaseCorrelateIterative` (per-patch FFTs), the NumPy FFT and Python-level loops, which COOL does not specifically accelerate; and the stock aarch64 wheel already ships KleidiCV (a newer version, 26.03, than COOL's 0.7.0), which narrows the gap that COOL's optimisations would otherwise open over a generic build.

## Caveats

- The OpenCV versions differ (5.1.0-dev vs 5.0.0), so a difference could come from the version as well as from COOL's optimisations. No same-version non-COOL build was available to isolate it.
- One instance, one run session, 2 vCPUs, Spot, a small clip. Not a statement about larger instances or other workloads; the listing's own claims concern functions such as resize, adaptive thresholding and contour detection, of which we measured only `resize` (faster) and `findContours` (no difference on our test image).
- Cost per 1,000 clips (compute only, at the Spot price above) is in the tables; it is about the same for both builds. The COOL software fee is expected to be $0 inside the 7-day trial; confirm in Billing.

## Reproduce

```bash
# on a c8g.large from the COOL AMI (check cv2.__version__ is 5.x first)
bash scripts/ec2_setup.sh                       # stock venv + the server extras; the COOL venv is root-owned and untouched
python scripts/verify_cool.py                   # in each environment (the scripts handle activation)
INSTANCE_TYPE=c8g.large AMI_ID=<ami> SPOT_PRICE=<$/h> bash scripts/run_bench_ec2.sh --width 480 --height 270 --dur 2
```

The COOL venv's `activate` script sets `PYTHONPATH` and `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` (COOL's cv2 and its bundled ffmpeg libraries); calling its `python` directly cannot import `cv2`.
